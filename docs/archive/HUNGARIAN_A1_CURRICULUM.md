# Hungarian A1 Curriculum Map

**Status:** Working curriculum specification  
**Level:** CEFR A1  
**Structure:** 40 instructional units + 3 review units  
**Language:** Hungarian (`hu`)

## Curriculum architecture

Each instructional unit contains:

- 5 lessons
- 25 exercises per lesson, including explanation, controlled practice, retrieval and review
- 1 reading
- 1 consolidation

The 3 review units consolidate earlier material and introduce no major new grammar.

### Scale

- 40 instructional units
- 3 review units
- 215 lessons total
- 5,375 exercises total
- 43 readings
- 43 consolidations
- Target: roughly 20–25 genuinely new core lexical items per instructional unit
- Target vocabulary: roughly 800–1,000 core lexical items by the end of A1

Vocabulary counts refer to lexical items/lemmas, not every inflected form. Common Hungarian inflections should be taught and recycled as productive morphology rather than counted as separate vocabulary items.

## Pedagogical principles

1. **Build the Hungarian system early.** Pronunciation, vowel harmony, articles, `van`, basic word order, possession, location and present-tense conjugation are foundations rather than late-course topics.
2. **Keep units narrow.** The 40-unit structure decomposes A1 content into manageable learning steps rather than increasing the amount of material learners must absorb at once.
3. **Recycle heavily.** New vocabulary and grammar should recur across subsequent units and readings.
4. **Teach morphology productively.** Case endings, possession and conjugation should be practised through meaningful examples rather than memorised as isolated tables.
5. **Treat definite and indefinite conjugation explicitly.** This is a central Hungarian system and deserves dedicated instruction.
6. **Do not make past tense an A1 production target.** Past-tense conjugation belongs to A2. Common forms such as `volt` may appear incidentally in input.
7. **Countries and nationalities are deliberately late.** They are useful A1 vocabulary but are not foundational to the grammar, so they appear near the end of the course.
8. **Do not attempt to teach every Hungarian case at A1.** Prioritise the high-frequency case system and common postpositions needed for everyday communication.
9. **Story continuity is part of the curriculum.** The recurring cast, relationships and established facts should remain consistent throughout the course.
10. **The story serves the language progression.** Characters and events should become richer over time, but narrative developments must not force grammar that has not yet been taught.
11. **Language transition is part of the story.** Meg begins with little or no Hungarian and is gradually introduced to the language alongside the learner. The proportion of Hungarian in the story increases as the learner's competence develops.
12. **English is scaffolding, not a permanent crutch.** Early story dialogue may use substantial English to establish context and make Hungarian comprehensible. English should progressively recede, especially through Units 1–10, until the story is predominantly Hungarian.

# Story canon

The Hungarian course uses the **same established cast and world as the Spanish course**. Do not invent a replacement cast.

| Character | Role |
|---|---|
| **Károly** | Hungarian man |
| **Meg** | South African woman |
| **András** | Károly's brother |
| **Anna** | Károly's sister |
| **Kaylee** | Meg's sister, twin |
| **Lauren** | Meg's sister, twin |
| **Mariann** | Károly and Meg's Hungarian friend |

Existing character biographies, relationships and established facts from the Spanish course should be preserved. Do not invent contradictory biographical details. New facts should only be introduced when they are useful to the story and should then remain consistent.

The story should develop across the A1 course rather than resetting at every unit. Each unit below therefore has a **main story beat** as well as its language objectives.


## Language-transition principle

The opening story deliberately mirrors Meg's experience of learning Hungarian.

- **Units 1–2:** The story can be substantially in English. Károly and the other Hungarian characters introduce short Hungarian expressions in context. Meg asks what things mean, giving the learner natural explanations.
- **Units 3–5:** English remains a significant support, but simple Hungarian exchanges become increasingly common. Meg begins producing short Hungarian utterances herself.
- **Units 6–10:** Hungarian becomes the dominant language of simple exchanges. English remains available for occasional clarification, misunderstandings and narrative context.
- **Units 11–20:** The story is predominantly Hungarian. English should be used sparingly and only when it serves a clear pedagogical or narrative purpose.
- **Units 21–30:** Story dialogue should normally be Hungarian, with contextual support rather than translation-heavy dialogue.
- **Units 31–40:** The learner should be operating almost entirely inside the Hungarian world. English may still appear in instructions or exceptional narrative situations, but it should no longer be a routine crutch.

The underlying principle is:

> **The learner and Meg enter Hungarian together.**

This progression should feel organic rather than mechanical. English recedes because Meg becomes more capable of understanding and participating in Hungarian.

# Phase I: Build the system

## Unit 1. Hungarian Sounds & Spelling

**Communicative goal:** Pronounce and recognise basic Hungarian words.

**Grammar/language:** alphabet; long/short vowels; long/short consonants; vowel harmony; stress; basic pronunciation rules; `a/az`; `egy`.

**Vocabulary:** greetings, classroom language and basic objects.

**Story beat:** Károly and Meg establish the setting largely in English. Károly introduces a few Hungarian greetings and sounds, and Meg begins noticing that she is going to have to learn the language.

## Unit 2. Greetings & Basic Interaction

**Communicative goal:** Greet people, say goodbye, thank, apologise and ask for repetition.

**Grammar/language:** formal/informal interaction; `te`/`ön`; basic question-answer patterns; basic negation.

**Vocabulary:** greetings, courtesy and survival expressions.

**Story beat:** Meg meets more of Károly's circle. The conversations are still largely in English, but Károly, András, Anna and Mariann begin using short Hungarian greetings and expressions with her.

## Unit 3. Introducing Yourself

**Communicative goal:** Say your name and basic personal information; ask another person's name and where they live.

**Grammar/language:** personal pronouns; `lenni`; `van`; nominal sentences; `Ki? Mi? Hol?`.

**Story beat:** The group gets to know one another more formally. Meg starts introducing herself in Hungarian, while English still carries much of the surrounding conversation.

## Unit 4. Numbers & Personal Information

**Communicative goal:** Give and ask for age, phone number, address and basic personal information.

**Grammar/language:** numbers; age constructions; question formation; basic word order.

**Story beat:** The characters exchange practical details and contact information. Meg manages a small amount of the interaction in Hungarian without Károly translating everything for her.

## Unit 5. What Is This?

**Communicative goal:** Identify and ask about everyday objects.

**Grammar/language:** `Mi ez? Mi az?`; definite/indefinite articles; demonstratives; introduction to plurals; `van`.

**Story beat:** Meg spends time with Károly and starts asking about everyday objects in Hungarian. English is now mainly used to clarify new words and situations.

## Unit 6. Family

**Communicative goal:** Introduce immediate family and say who someone is.

**Grammar/language:** possessive introduction; possession with `van`; singular/plural nouns; third-person structures.

**Story beat:** The wider family becomes part of the story. Meg can now follow simple Hungarian descriptions of András and Anna and begin talking about Kaylee and Lauren herself.

## Unit 7. Describing People

**Communicative goal:** Describe appearance and basic character.

**Grammar/language:** adjectives; adjective + noun; adjective position; plural descriptions; basic intensification.

**Story beat:** The characters describe one another, including the twins Kaylee and Lauren, giving the learner a reason to distinguish people clearly.

## Unit 8. People, Objects & Plurals

**Communicative goal:** Describe what exists and how many there are.

**Grammar/language:** `-k`; plural formation; demonstratives; adjective + plural noun; quantity expressions.

**Story beat:** A group gathering brings several characters together. The scene is now mostly in simple Hungarian, with English appearing only when Meg needs clarification.

## Unit 9. Possession

**Communicative goal:** Say what you have and who something belongs to.

**Grammar/language:** possessive suffixes; `-ja/-je`; `-a/-e`; possessive structures; Hungarian possession/'have' construction.

**Story beat:** The characters deal with personal belongings and shared objects. Meg is increasingly able to follow the conversation directly in Hungarian, marking the end of the initial transition arc.

## Review 1. Foundations

Consolidate Units 1–9. No major new grammar.

**Story beat:** The first story arc brings the group together in a simple social situation where the learner must identify people, describe them, discuss family and talk about possessions.

# Phase II: Location, verbs & everyday life

## Unit 11. Where Things Are

**Grammar/language:** `-ban/-ben`; `van`; `Hol?`; location sentences.

**Vocabulary:** rooms, classroom and common places.

**Story beat:** The characters spend time at home and in familiar places, and Meg begins describing where people and objects are.

## Unit 12. Going Somewhere

**Grammar/language:** `-ba/-be`; movement; `Hová?`; location vs destination.

**Story beat:** Meg goes somewhere with Károly and has to distinguish where they are from where they are going.

## Unit 13. What You Do

**Grammar/language:** present-tense indefinite conjugation; common verb stems; affirmative sentences; subject omission.

**Vocabulary:** high-frequency everyday verbs.

**Story beat:** The learner begins following the characters' ordinary activities. Károly, Meg, András and Anna talk about what they do during the day.

## Unit 14. Saying No & Asking Questions

**Grammar/language:** `nem`; yes/no questions; question words; verb position; sentence transformations.

**Story beat:** A small misunderstanding gives the characters a reason to ask and answer questions, correct information and say what they do or do not do.

## Unit 15. Daily Routine

**Communicative goal:** Talk about a typical day.

**Grammar/language:** present-tense consolidation; sequencing activities; common routine expressions.

**Vocabulary:** waking, eating, working, studying, sleeping and related routine verbs.

**Story beat:** The learner follows Károly and Meg through a normal day and compares their routines.

## Unit 16. Time & Dates

**Grammar/language:** clock time; days; months; parts of day; `mikor?`; time expressions.

**Story beat:** The group needs to coordinate a meeting, so time and dates become practically important.

## Unit 17. Frequency & Word Order

**Grammar/language:** `mindig`, `gyakran`, `néha`, `ritkán`, `soha`; basic Hungarian focus/word order; frequency placement.

**Story beat:** The characters discuss habits and routines, including things they always, often or rarely do together.

## Unit 18. The Home

**Grammar/language:** locative review; possessives in context; basic postpositions; adjective + noun combinations.

**Vocabulary:** rooms, furniture and household objects.

**Story beat:** Meg spends time in Károly's home and the learner gets a clearer picture of the characters' everyday domestic environment.

## Unit 19. Food & Drink

**Grammar/language:** quantities; basic object marking; requests; count/quantity concepts.

**Vocabulary:** staple foods, drinks, meals and ingredients.

**Story beat:** The characters prepare or share a meal, introducing food as a recurring social activity.

## Review 2. Everyday Hungarian

Consolidate Units 11–19.

**Story beat:** The characters arrange and spend a day together, requiring the learner to combine location, movement, routines, time, home and food language.

# Phase III: Everyday transactions

## Unit 21. Buying Food

**Grammar/language:** accusative `-t`; quantities; prices; requests.

**Communicative goal:** Buy food in a shop or market.

**Story beat:** Meg goes shopping with or for Károly and has to make her first substantial food purchase in Hungarian.

## Unit 22. At the Market

**Grammar/language:** accusative consolidation; demonstratives; quantity expressions; `mennyi?`; `hány?`.

**Vocabulary:** produce, meat, dairy, packaging, weights and measures.

**Story beat:** Károly and Meg visit a market, where they choose ingredients and discuss quantities and prices.

## Unit 23. At the Café

**Grammar/language:** polite requests; `szeretnék`; `kérek`; accusative consolidation.

**Communicative goal:** Order food and drinks and make simple requests.

**Story beat:** The group meets at a café. Meg takes a more active role in ordering in Hungarian.

## Unit 24. At the Restaurant

**Grammar/language:** `szeretne`; `kell`; `és`, `de`, `mert`; requests and preferences.

**Communicative goal:** Order a meal, understand a simple menu and pay.

**Story beat:** A group meal brings Károly, Meg, András, Anna and Mariann together and introduces a more extended social interaction.

## Unit 25. Shopping

**Grammar/language:** accusative consolidation; demonstratives; adjective position; polite questions.

**Communicative goal:** Ask about products, prices and sizes.

**Story beat:** Meg and one of the others go shopping for something practical, requiring questions about products and choices.

## Unit 26. Clothes & Appearance

**Communicative goal:** Describe clothing, ask for a size/colour and buy clothes.

**Vocabulary:** clothes, colours, sizes and common materials.

**Story beat:** The characters discuss what they are wearing and help one another choose clothes.

## Unit 27. The City

**Grammar/language:** places in town; location review; common place suffixes; `Hol? Hová?`.

**Vocabulary:** station, bank, shop, pharmacy, school, post office and other essential places.

**Story beat:** Meg starts navigating the city more independently and asks where familiar places are.

## Unit 28. Transport & Directions

**Grammar/language:** `-val/-vel`; basic movement; `jön`, `megy`; direction questions; basic imperative/request forms.

**Communicative goal:** Use public transport and ask for/give simple directions.

**Story beat:** Meg and Mariann travel across the city, with a minor navigation problem requiring directions and transport language.

## Unit 29. Hobbies & Free Time

**Grammar/language:** present-tense consolidation; `szeret`; `szeretne`; expressing preferences.

**Vocabulary:** sport, films, music, reading, going out and other leisure activities.

**Story beat:** The characters talk about their interests and discover activities they enjoy doing together.

## Unit 30. Friends & Making Plans

**Grammar/language:** invitations; appointments; `akar`; `szeretne`; `lehet`; time expressions.

**Communicative goal:** Invite someone, accept/refuse and arrange a time.

**Story beat:** The group makes plans for a shared activity, creating a natural reason to negotiate time and invitations.

# Phase IV: Expand the Hungarian system

## Unit 31. Definite & Indefinite Objects

**Grammar/language:** definite vs indefinite objects; when the object is definite; when it is indefinite; accusative review; relationship between object type and conjugation.

This unit establishes the conceptual distinction before full definite conjugation.

**Story beat:** Everyday conversations become more precise as the characters refer to specific and non-specific things they are doing, buying or looking for.

## Unit 32. Definite Conjugation

**Grammar/language:** present definite conjugation; comparison with indefinite conjugation; common verb patterns; object/conjugation interaction.

**Story beat:** The learner can now follow more precise conversations between the characters about what they are doing with particular objects.

## Unit 33. Ability, Possibility & Necessity

**Grammar/language:** `tud`; `lehet`; `kell`; infinitive `-ni`.

**Communicative goal:** Say what you can, cannot, must and need to do.

**Story beat:** The characters encounter a practical problem and discuss what each person can, must or needs to do.

## Unit 34. Wants, Preferences & Requests

**Grammar/language:** `akar`; `szeret`; `szeretne`; infinitive; polite requests; modal consolidation.

**Story beat:** The characters express competing preferences while organising an activity, giving the learner a reason to distinguish wants, wishes and polite requests.

## Unit 35. People, Things & Relationships

**Grammar/language:** possessive review; personal pronouns; `-nak/-nek`; basic dative meaning; common relationship constructions.

**Story beat:** Relationships within the group become more explicit as the characters discuss who they are doing things for and who is connected to whom.

## Unit 36. With, From & About

**Grammar/language:** `-val/-vel`; `-ból/-ből`; `-ról/-ről`; `-tól/-től`; common postpositions; case selection through meaning.

**Story beat:** The characters discuss where things came from, what they are talking about and who they are doing things with.

## Unit 37. Movement & Place

**Grammar/language:** three-direction system consolidation; `-ban/-ben`; `-ba/-be`; `-ból/-ből`; `-on/-en/-ön`; `-ra/-re`; `-ról/-ről`; common postpositions.

**Communicative goal:** Describe where things are, where they go and where they come from.

**Story beat:** A more complicated journey brings together the location and movement systems the learner has accumulated.

## Unit 38. Weather & Seasons

**Grammar/language:** impersonal weather expressions; temperature; basic comparisons; time/season expressions.

**Vocabulary:** weather, seasons, months, clothing and activities.

**Story beat:** A change in weather affects the group's plans, giving the characters a reason to discuss seasons, conditions and what they should do.

## Unit 39. Countries, Nationalities & Languages

**Communicative goal:** Say where someone is from, what languages they speak and discuss countries and cities.

**Grammar/language:** country/place names; `-ból/-ből`; `-ban/-ben`; language expressions; nationality terms.

This topic is deliberately late even though some external A1 curricula introduce it earlier. It is useful vocabulary but is not foundational to the core Hungarian grammar sequence.

**Story beat:** Meg's South African background and the group's different connections to Hungary naturally lead to a conversation about countries, languages and where people come from.

## Unit 40. Travel & Accommodation

**Communicative goal:** Buy/ask about tickets, check into accommodation, give basic personal information and handle simple travel problems.

**Grammar/language:** comprehensive A1 review; location/direction; possession; definite/indefinite conjugation; requests and modal verbs.

**Story beat:** The A1 story culminates in a trip involving Károly, Meg and the wider group, requiring the learner to use the accumulated language in travel and accommodation situations.

# Review 3. A1 Survival & Consolidation

Consolidate Units 21–40 through integrated tasks.

**Story beat:** The final review brings the recurring cast together and revisits the major situations of the course, allowing the learner to demonstrate practical A1 Hungarian across a continuous social context.

Learners should be able to:

- shop and order food
- navigate a city
- use basic public transport
- make plans and invitations
- discuss hobbies
- express ability, necessity and desire
- use basic definite and indefinite conjugation
- use common case endings
- discuss countries and languages
- handle basic travel situations

# A1 grammar boundary

## Core A1 systems

- Hungarian alphabet and pronunciation
- vowel harmony
- long/short vowels and consonants
- definite/indefinite articles
- personal pronouns
- `van` / `lenni`
- nominal sentences
- negation
- question formation
- basic Hungarian word order and focus
- plural formation
- possession
- present indefinite conjugation
- accusative
- `-ban/-ben`, `-ba/-be`, `-ból/-ből`
- `-on/-en/-ön`, `-ra/-re`, `-ról/-ről`
- `-val/-vel`
- `-nak/-nek`
- selected postpositions
- definite vs indefinite objects
- present definite conjugation
- infinitive `-ni`
- `tud`, `lehet`, `kell`, `akar`, `szeret`, `szeretne`
- common irregular verbs such as `jön` and `megy`
- limited high-frequency verbal-prefix exposure

## Explicitly A2

- productive past-tense conjugation
- full future-tense system
- broader verbal-prefix system
- advanced case usage
- complex subordinate clauses
- advanced word-order/focus patterns

Past forms such as `volt` may occur incidentally in A1 input, but learners are not expected to master productive past-tense conjugation until A2.

# Vocabulary policy

Target approximately **20–25 new core lexical items per instructional unit**, giving roughly **800–1,000 core lexical items** across the full A1 course.

Do not count every inflected form as a separate vocabulary item. A single lexical item should generate meaningful practice with common forms where appropriate, for example:

- `ház`
- `házak`
- `házat`
- `házban`
- `házba`
- `házból`
- `házam`

The learner needs to recognise and produce the morphology, but these should not inflate the vocabulary target artificially.

Verbs and high-frequency functional language should receive disproportionate attention because they drive productive A1 communication.

# Unit lesson architecture

Every unit follows the same broad architecture, while individual lesson objectives vary according to the grammar and topic:

1. **Lesson 1:** introduce the unit's new language and establish meaning
2. **Lesson 2:** controlled grammar and vocabulary practice
3. **Lesson 3:** integration and manipulation of the target language
4. **Lesson 4:** communicative/application practice
5. **Lesson 5:** retrieval, recycling and preparation for consolidation
6. **Reading:** contextualised A1 text using the unit's target language and previously learned material
7. **Consolidation:** integrated demonstration of the unit's communicative and grammatical objectives

Each lesson contains 25 exercises. Exercise count includes explanation/review activities where the lesson format calls for them. The 25-exercise count should not be interpreted as 25 new pieces of material.

# External cross-check

The curriculum was cross-checked against:

- **University of Vienna Hungarian:** explicitly CEFR-aligned; uses MagyarOK A1+ and identifies pronunciation, vowel harmony, articles, `van`, possession, present conjugation, locative cases, definite/indefinite conjugation, modal verbs, transport, food, work/study and everyday communication across A1.
- **University of Pécs Hungarian for Foreigners:** uses MagyarOK A1+ and covers introductions, family, food, restaurants, clothes, routines, leisure, appointments and weather as A1 communicative content.
- **BME University Beginners' Hungarian:** provides A1.1/A1.2 outcomes covering pronunciation, vowel harmony, articles, numbers, word order, plurals, conjugation, possession, case usage and everyday communication.
- **European Commission OLS My Hungarian Course A1:** provides an independent A1 sequence covering introductions, family, appearance, routine, work/volunteering, neighbourhood, transport, food, shopping, home, cafés, social situations and other everyday needs.

The external curricula generally introduce countries/nationalities earlier than this curriculum does. That is an intentional Parlour sequencing choice, not a coverage omission.

The external curricula vary in how much past-tense material they expose at A1. Parlour deliberately keeps **productive past-tense conjugation at A2** in order to protect the A1 foundation and reduce grammar overload.
