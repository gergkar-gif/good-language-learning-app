Genre: capstone essay
This is the closing unit of C1 core (the reviewer wrote these notes). It teaches no new skill: every lesson brings together C1 skills taught in Units 1-25, the way B2 Unit 36 closed B2. Screens open with "You know *X* from C1 Unit N" (take N from the knowledge sheet or `python scripts/c1_recap_candidates.py`; for C1 skills, the unit whose screen is the skill's `taught_in` in `skills/es.json`) and show how the forms work together in the learner's own writing and speech. Items are tagged with those C1 skills or `c1-own-voice-c1-vocab`.
Lesson plan:
- 01 writing with precision and density: `infinitivo-compuesto-nominal` (C1 Unit 1), `relativos-el-cual` (C1 Unit 7) and `derivacion-nominal-verbal` (C1 Unit 3) together in one argued paragraph (*Tras haber analizado los datos, la comisión, la cual …*).
- 02 correct and careful written Spanish: `dequeismo-queismo` and `gerundio-normativo` (C1 Unit 2) and `concordancia-ad-sensum` (C1 Unit 8): editing a short paragraph of one's own (*pienso que*, not *pienso de que*; the gerund of a later action rewritten).
- 03 meaning through mood and order: `verbos-doble-modo` (C1 Unit 12) and `dislocacion-clitica` (C1 Unit 11): saying exactly what one means (*Siento que tengas que irte* vs *Siento que algo cambia*; *Ese argumento lo comparto*).
- 04 conceding, evaluating, being ironic: `futuro-concesivo` and `con-lo-que-para-lo-que` (C1 Unit 20) and `ni-que-exclamativo` (C1 Unit 21).
- 05 one's own voice in speech: `marcadores-conversacionales` (C1 Unit 18), `sufijos-apreciativos` (C1 Unit 22) and `se-aspectual` (C1 Unit 17): the same idea said formally and informally; plus the text and the Reading group.
The recognition-only skills (`futuro-subjuntivo-reconocimiento`, `leismo-laismo-reconocimiento`) and the press skills (`condicional-de-rumor`, `estilo-titular`) are not drilled here; one Reading or listening item may recognise them.
Domain words: writing and voice (*el estilo* only if no earlier unit lists it, *la voz propia, el registro* only if not listed, *pulir* only if not listed, *la claridad, matizar* only if not listed, *expresarse*); most words are already known: list only what is new.
Text: a capstone essay by an invented language learner (unnamed, *una estudiante*, not Meg) on what it means to have one's own voice in a second language: precision, register, irony, knowing the norm and its varieties. It uses many C1 forms naturally, never as a list. No real person, study or quotation.
