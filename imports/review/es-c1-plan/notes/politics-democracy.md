Genre: speech
This unit teaches no new skill: every lesson recaps known B1/B2 skills at C1 density in politics, civic institutions, and democratic debate (screens open with "You know *X* from <Level> Unit N", the unit number taken from the knowledge sheet, and add what is new at C1: how civic and parliamentary speeches use the form). Items are tagged with those skills or `c1-politics-democracy-vocab`.
Lesson plan:
- 01 expressing formal recommendations and oratorical counterfactuals: recap `apodosis-condicional-literaria` (B2 Unit 9) — extending B2 Unit 9's *quisiera*, *pudiera*, and *debiera* in place of the conditional to civic speeches: plural institutional openings (*quisiéramos subrayar*, *debiera garantizar el bien común*) and recognizing compound *hubiera* + participle in the result clause of formal counterfactuals (*si el pleno hubiera deliberado antes, hubiera evitado el bloqueo*), while noting that *-se* (*quisiese*, *hubiese*) is not used in place of the conditional.
- 02 pivoting between concession and condition in public debate: recap `contrast-concession-connectors` (B1 Unit 1) — extending B1 Unit 1's *sin embargo* and *no obstante* to parenthetical mid-clause placement (*la coalición, no obstante, necesita diálogo social*) and adding formal parliamentary pivots: *con todo* ('even so') and *ahora bien* ('now then / that said', introducing a crucial condition or caveat after a concession).
- 03 marking legislative transitions and shifts of responsibility: recap `perifrasis-pasar-a` (B2 Unit 23) — extending B2 Unit 23's *pasar a* + infinitive to institutional speeches and legislative procedures: passive/impersonal transitions (*el proyecto de ley pasa a ser debatido en comisión*), fixed institutional frames (*pasar a formar parte de*, *pasar a depender de*), and *pasemos a* + infinitive to move an assembly to the next item on the agenda.
- 04 voicing democratic inclusivity across differences and past outcomes: recap `reduplicated-subjunctive` (B2 Unit 8) — extending B2 Unit 8's present-subjunctive formulas (*pase lo que pase*, *sea como sea*) to open civic verbs (*gane quien gane*, *vote quien vote*), agreeing *sea cual sea / sean cuales sean* + noun (*sean cuales sean las diferencias*), and past-tense imperfect subjunctive reduplication (*fuera cual fuera el escrutinio*, *dijeran lo que dijeran*).
- 05 structuring and closing a civic address: recap `argument-markers` (B1 Unit 13) — extending B1 Unit 13's *en resumen* and *lo más importante es que* to formal speech architecture: ordering pillars (*en primer término*, *en segundo lugar*, *por último*) and closing a democratic address (*en definitiva*, *en suma*) to link diagnosis to civic commitment; plus the unit speech and the Reading group.
Domain words: politics, civic institutions, and democracy (*la deliberación, deliberar, el pleno, la moción, el quórum, el bien común, el disenso, la coalición, la oposición, la polarización, el diálogo social, la alternancia, la legislatura, legislar, el proyecto de ley, el veto, la derogación, la separación de poderes, el electorado, el escrutinio, la abstención, el escaño, el concejal, la representación, la participación ciudadana, el referendo, la consulta popular, cívico, la convivencia democrática, la agenda pública*); verify each against `.scratch/sheet-politics-democracy.md` before writing so no earlier lemma is re-listed.
Text: an opening civic address to an invented municipal assembly at the start of a new legislative term—calling for respectful deliberation, institutional checks and balances, dialogue between majority coalitions and the opposition, and wider citizen participation through public consultations. General, uncontroversial democratic principles hedged where they are judgements; no real person, living politician, party, government, country, quotation, date, or statistic.
