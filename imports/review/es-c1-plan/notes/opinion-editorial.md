Genre: editorial
This unit teaches no new skill: every lesson recaps known B1/B2 skills at C1 density in opinion writing (screens open with "You know *X* from B2 Unit N" and add what is new at C1: how an editorial uses the form). Items are tagged with those skills or `c1-opinion-editorial-vocab`.
Lesson plan:
- 01 taking a position: recap `stance-adverbs` (B2): *evidentemente*, *sin duda*, *a todas luces*, *francamente*.
- 02 rejecting a reading and offering another: recap `refutacion-no-es-que-sino` (B2): *No es que la medida sea mala, sino que llega tarde.*
- 03 judging with the subjunctive: recap `subjunctive-value-judgments` (A2) and `el-hecho-de-que-subjuntivo` (B2) at C1 register: *Resulta inadmisible que…*, *Es de lamentar que…*, *El hecho de que…*.
- 04 conceding and still disagreeing: recap `concesivas-por-mas-que`, `concesivas-si-bien`, `concesivas-aun-cuando` (B2).
- 05 rhetoric in an editorial: recap `rhetorical-devices` (B2): rhetorical questions, lists of three, a closing call; plus the text and the Reading group.
Domain words: opinion and public debate (e.g. *el editorial, la postura, la medida, la ciudadanía, cuestionar, defender, lamentar, inadmisible*); check each against the sheet first.
Text: an editorial of an invented newspaper on whether a city should close its historic centre to private cars: it takes a clear side, concedes the strongest objection, rejects a misreading, and ends with a call. No real city, mayor, study or statistic.
