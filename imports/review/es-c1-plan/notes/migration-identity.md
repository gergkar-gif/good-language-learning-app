Genre: testimony (*crónica*)
This unit teaches no new skill: every lesson recaps known B1/B2 skills at C1 density in migration, belonging, and cultural identity (screens open with "You know *X* from <Level> Unit N", the unit number taken from the knowledge sheet, and add what is new at C1: how testimony and *crónica* prose use the form). Items are tagged with those skills or `c1-migration-identity-vocab`.
Lesson plan:
- 01 narrating stages of departure and settlement: recap `conectores-temporales-narrativos` (B2 Unit 2) — extending B2 Unit 2's past-indicative narrative connectors (*apenas*, *en cuanto*, *al cabo de*, *a medida que*, *conforme*) to C1 chronicle prose: pairing *apenas* with the pluperfect (*apenas había terminado la travesía cuando empezó a buscar trabajo*), using uncounted *al cabo de los años*, and contrasting past indicative (*conforme pasaban los años, se fue arraigando*) with prospective subjunctive (*conforme vayan llegando nuevas familias*, *apenas reciban la resolución del consulado*).
- 02 tracing gradual adaptation and inherited traditions: recap `gerund-periphrases` (B2 Unit 24) — extending B2 Unit 24's *ir*, *andar*, and *venir* + gerund to *se* passive/impersonal periphrases with verb-subject agreement (*se van mezclando los acentos*, *se vienen conservando estas recetas desde hace décadas*, *se fueron tejiendo redes de acogida*) and contrasting clitic placement (*ir adaptándose* vs *irse adaptando*).
- 03 expressing hard-won belonging and cautious understatement: recap `perifrasis-llegar-a-infinitivo` (B2 Unit 25) — extending B2 Unit 25's *llegar a* + infinitive to testimony: negated *no llegar a* + infinitive as quiet understatement (*nunca llegó a perder la lengua materna*, *no llegaron a sentirse forasteros*), imperfect *llegaba a* for recurring extremes, and pluperfect *había llegado a* for a stage reached before a later turning point.
- 04 expressing persistence across distance and time: recap `concesivas-por-mas-que` (B2 Unit 8) — extending B2 Unit 8's *por más que* / *por mucho que* + verb and *por muy* + adjective + *que* to noun-phrase concessives with agreeing quantifiers (*por muchos años que pasen*, *por más trámites que pida el consulado*, *por poca acogida que encontraran al llegar*), contrasting general or prospective subjunctive with factual past indicative.
- 05 voicing longing, nostalgia, and unreal wishes in testimony: recap `counterfactual-wishes` (B2 Unit 16) — extending B2 Unit 16's *ojalá* and *¡quién hubiera!* + pluperfect subjunctive to C1 testimony: contrasting unreal present wishes (*¡quién pudiera caminar de nuevo por ese puerto!*) with past regrets (*¡quién hubiera grabado aquellas canciones!*, *si al menos hubiéramos conservado las cartas*), and weaving wistful exclamations into a *crónica*; plus the unit testimony (*crónica*) and the Reading group.
Domain words: migration, belonging, and identity (*el migrante, migrar, la acogida, acoger, la añoranza, el apego, arraigarse, echar raíces, la travesía, el reencuentro, la lengua materna, el bilingüismo, la asimilación, el consulado, la nacionalidad, el refugio, el forastero, la patria, retornar, el acento*); verify each against `.scratch/sheet-migration-identity.md` before writing so no earlier lemma is re-listed.
Text: a first-person *crónica* / testimony set in an invented coastal port city where generations of families have arrived from inland towns and neighbouring valleys—tracing how a neighbourhood bakery and Sunday table keep a mother tongue, an accent, and small rituals alive while children grow up bilingual and build a dual sense of home. General, uncontroversial reflections on belonging and memory, hedged where they are judgements; no real person, institution, study, statistic, or country.
