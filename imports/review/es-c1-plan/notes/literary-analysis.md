Genre: essay on a genre, not one work
This unit teaches no new skill: every lesson recaps known B1/B2 skills at C1 density in literature, narrative technique, and literary analysis (screens open with "You know *X* from <Level> Unit N", the unit number taken from the knowledge sheet, and add what is new at C1: how literary essays and close reading use the form). Items are tagged with those skills or `c1-literary-analysis-vocab`.
Lesson plan:
- 01 producing and analysing free indirect discourse: recap `discurso-indirecto-libre` (B2 Unit 17) — moving beyond B2 Unit 17's recognition-only introduction to active transformation and critical analysis: converting direct or reported thought into free indirect discourse with imperfect, pluperfect, and conditional verbs plus character-anchored deictics (*ahora*, *mañana*, *aquí*), and explaining how the merged voice creates narrative irony or unreliability.
- 02 framing suspense and averted turning points: recap `estar-a-punto-de` (B1 Unit 1) — extending B1 Unit 1's interrupted imperfect (*estaba a punto de salir cuando sonó el teléfono*) to literary analysis: preterite *estuvo a punto de* + infinitive for a dramatic threshold narrowly averted (*el protagonista estuvo a punto de delatarse*), and critical-present commentary (*la trama parece estar a punto de resolverse cuando irrumpe la elipsis*).
- 03 using litotes and critical understatement: recap `dejar-de-infinitivo` (B1 Unit 12) — extending B1 Unit 12's habit cessation (*dejé de fumar*) to formal literary essays: *no dejar de ser* + noun/adjective for measured critical judgement (*el recurso no deja de ser eficaz*), and *no dejar de* + verb of attention (*no deja de sorprender la brevedad del desenlace*).
- 04 tracing motif recurrence and rereading: recap `volver-a-infinitivo` (B2 Unit 23) — extending B2 Unit 23's everyday repetition (*volvió a llover*) to structural analysis of fiction: motif return (*el símbolo vuelve a surgir en el cierre*), impersonal rereading (*el primer párrafo se vuelve a leer con otra clave*), and contrasting *volver a* + infinitive with *volver sobre* + noun ('to revisit a theme or motif').
- 05 analysing plot culmination and thematic convergence: recap `terminar-por-infinitivo` (B2 Unit 25) — extending B2 Unit 25's personal culmination (*terminó por aceptar*) to abstract literary subjects (*la ambigüedad termina por imponerse*, *el silencio acaba por revelar la verdad*) and contrasting *terminar por* + infinitive ('to end up') with *terminar de* + infinitive ('to finish'); plus the unit essay on the short story genre and the Reading group.
Domain words: literature, narrative craft, and literary analysis (*el cuento, el relato, el narrador, el protagonista, la trama, el desenlace, la elipsis, la metáfora, el símbolo, el monólogo, la ironía, la ambigüedad, el verosímil / verosímil, la brevedad, el suspenso, el giro, el recurso, el estilo, el género, la prosa, la ficción, la voz narrativa, el subtexto, la pista, releer, sugerir, insinuar, revelar, delatar, entrelazar*); verify each against `.scratch/sheet-literary-analysis.md` before writing so no earlier lemma is re-listed.
Text: a literary essay on the short story genre (*el cuento breve*)—examining how brevity, ellipsis, unreliable narrators, recurring motifs, and understated endings invite rereading, illustrated only with invented schematic examples. General, uncontroversial literary observations hedged where they are judgements; no real author, real work, plot retelling, quotation, date, or statistic.
