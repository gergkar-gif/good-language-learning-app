Lesson plan:
- 01 layering historical chronology and archival anteriority: recap `pluscuamperfecto` (B1 Unit 1) — extending B1 Unit 1's everyday sequence to historical layering, passive pluperfect (*había sido demolido*, *se había conservado*), and negative anteriority before a turning point (*antes de la demolición, los vecinos ya habían redactado un inventario*; *los cronistas ignoraban lo que había ocurrido décadas atrás*).
- 02 states, discoveries, and refusals in historical testimony: recap `verbos-cambio-significado-pasado` (B2 Unit 2) — applying *saber*, *conocer*, *querer*, and *poder* in the imperfect vs preterite to archival research and oral history, and contrasting deliberate institutional refusal (*el archivo no quiso ceder los manuscritos*) with structural inability (*el municipio no pudo restaurar la fachada*; *los historiadores supieron la fecha exacta al revisar la hemeroteca*).
- 03 reading the historiographical *-ra* alongside true subjunctive *-ra*: recap `subjuntivo-imperfecto-estilistico-ra` (B2 Unit 15) — extending B2 Unit 15's recognition of indicative-value *-ra* (*el edificio que albergara la hemeroteca*, *como señalara el cronista*) to distinguish factual indicative *-ra* (where *-se* is excluded) from true subjunctive *-ra* in concessive, hypothetical, or non-specific clauses (*aunque conservara las actas*, *buscaban a alguien que recordara el barrio*) within the same historical prose.
- 04 counterfactual history and retrospective evaluation: recap `pluperfect-subjunctive-forms` (B2 Unit 16) — extending B2 Unit 16's paradigm to historiographical counterfactuals and past-tense evaluations of earlier events (*si se hubiera catalogado el inventario, el manuscrito no se habría perdido*; *lamentaban que no se hubiesen recogido los testimonios*; *nadie imaginaba que la inscripción hubiera trascendido*).
- 05 witness voice and narrator voice in historical chronicles: recap `discurso-indirecto-libre` (B2 Unit 17) — extending B2 Unit 17's narrative recognition to historical essays and chronicles of memory, contrasting the historian's analytical voice with a community's or witness's unquoted perspective (*Los vecinos miraban el solar vacío. ¿Qué quedaría ahora de la antigua plaza? Nadie volvería a escuchar aquellas historias.*); plus the unit essay and the Reading group.
Domain words: history, archives, and collective memory (*la placa, la conmemoración, evocar, rememorar, el manuscrito, la hemeroteca, la historiografía, el antepasado, la inscripción, la demolición, trascender, catalogar, la posteridad, el inventario, la efeméride, la cronología, el solar, legar, testimoniar, la memoria colectiva*); all verified absent from earlier vocabulary files.
Text: an essay on how cities remember what they demolish—comparing official commemorations, bronze plaques, and municipal archives with fragile oral testimony and the empty plots (*solares*) left behind by vanished markets and workshops. General, uncontroversial reflections hedged where they are judgements; no real person, institution, study, statistic, or country.
