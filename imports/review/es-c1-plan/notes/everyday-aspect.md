Genre: short story with dialogue
Lesson plan:
- 01 NEW `se-aspectual`, screen `c1-17-01-a-gr`: the *se* of completeness with verbs of consuming, learning and perceiving: *comerse, beberse, tomarse, leerse, aprenderse, saberse, fumarse*. It marks that the action was done to the end on a whole, definite amount: *Se comió la tarta entera*, *Me leí el libro en un día*, *Se sabe todos los nombres*. Without a definite or quantified object it sounds wrong (*Se comió pan* is odd; *Comió pan* is the plain form). It is not reflexive (no one eats himself) and not passive. Common in speech in all varieties; less usual in formal writing. Items: the *se* form needs a whole, definite object in the sentence; wrong options are *se* with a bare noun (*Se bebió agua* for "drank some water") or the reflexive misread. At least 6 items tagged with it.
- 02 the accidental *se* (*Se me olvidaron las llaves*, *Se le cayó el vaso*): no registry skill teaches it, so present it as known only if the knowledge sheet shows a screen with it; tag items `pronombres-combinados` (A2) when a wrong answer shows the pronoun, otherwise the unit vocabulary skill. Report in "For the user" whether the course ever taught it.
- 03 colloquial reactions: recap `subjunctive-emotion` (A2) at C1 density (*¡Qué raro que no haya llamado!*).
- 04 habits and changes: recap `habitos-soler` (B1) and `dejar-de-infinitivo` (B1): *Suele tomarse un café*, *Dejó de fumar*.
- 05 telling an anecdote: recap `estar-a-punto-de` (B1) and `volver-a-infinitivo` (B2): *Estuve a punto de…*, *volvió a llamar*; plus the text and the Reading group.
Domain words: everyday life and food (*el bocado, de un trago, entero, la receta* only if not listed, *atragantarse, merendar*); check each against the sheet.
Text: a short story with dialogue about Meg and a friend (unnamed: *su amiga*) cooking a recipe for a party in Budapest that goes wrong and is saved at the last minute; Meg is a student, Carlos may appear (he works in an office). Uses aspectual *se* naturally. No real person.
