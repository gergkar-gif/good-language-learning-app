Genre: column
Lesson plan:
- 01 NEW `futuro-concesivo`, screen `c1-20-01-a-gr`: the future that concedes a point before a *pero*: *Será caro, pero vale la pena* (= it may well be expensive, but…); for the past, the conditional: *Tendría razón, pero no supo explicarlo*. You know the future and conditional of probability from B1 (`future-probability`): this is the same form conceding rather than guessing, and the *pero* clause is what shows it. Items need the *pero* clause so the concessive reading is clear. At least 6 items tagged with it.
- 02 concession recap: `a-pesar-de` (B1) and `concesivas-aun-cuando` (B2) contrasted with the concessive future.
- 03 NEW `con-lo-que-para-lo-que`, screen `c1-20-03-a-gr`: *con lo* + adjective/adverb + *que* + verb, and *con lo que* + verb: "considering how much…", often in complaint or surprise: *Con lo cansado que está, sigue trabajando*, *Con lo que estudió, aprobó por poco*; *para lo* + adjective + *que*: "for how … it is": *Para lo poco que cobra, trabaja muchísimo*. You know *lo* + adjective + *que* from B1 Unit 40 (*lo difícil que es*): recap it; the new part is *con* / *para* in front and what they mean. At least 6 items tagged with it.
- 04 evaluating with the subjunctive: recap `subjunctive-value-judgments` (A2) at C1 density.
- 05 balanced judgement: recap `contrast-markers` (B2); plus the text and the Reading group.
Domain words: everyday evaluation (*valer la pena* only if not listed, *exagerado, el lujo, quejarse* only if not listed, *merecer, de sobra*); check each against the sheet.
Text: a column in an invented newspaper on whether an expensive new city tram is worth it: it concedes with the future (*será caro, pero…*), evaluates with *con lo que / para lo que*. No real city or person.
