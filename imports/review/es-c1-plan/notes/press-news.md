Genre: news report
Lesson plan:
- 01 NEW `condicional-de-rumor`, screen `c1-04-01-a-gr`: the conditional (simple and perfect) to report information the writer has not confirmed: *El ministro habría dimitido*, *Los daños superarían el millón*. Markers that go with it: *según fuentes*, *al parecer*, *presuntamente*. Contrast with the known conditional of probability about the past (`future-probability`: *serían las diez* = probably) and the hypothetical conditional: here the meaning is "reportedly". Why: it lets a journalist pass on a claim without asserting it. Say plainly that style guides of some newspapers advise against it and prefer an explicit source ("según la policía") (true, but name no newspaper). Tip: English "reportedly / is said to have". At least 6 items tagged with it; wrong options must be clearly wrong (an indicative that asserts the fact as certain when the question asks for the unconfirmed version, pinned in the prompt).
- 02 reporting what was said in the news: recap `reporting-verbs` (B2) and `discurso-indirecto-deicticos` (B2): *afirmó que*, *negó que* + subjunctive, *al día siguiente*.
- 03 NEW `estilo-titular`, screen `c1-04-03-a-gr`: how headlines are written: the present for an event that already happened (*Dimite el alcalde*), the infinitive or *a* + infinitive for an announced future step only if you are sure of the use (else leave it out), verbless headlines (*Alerta por lluvias en la costa*), dropped articles, a colon to attribute words (*Alcalde: «No habrá recortes»*). Describe these as common conventions, not rules. At least 6 items tagged with it (match a headline with the full sentence it stands for, choose the headline form of a sentence).
- 04 passives in the news: recap `choosing-passive-type` (B2): *fue detenido*, *se detuvo a dos personas*, *las calles están cortadas*.
- 05 attribution and distance: recap `hedging` (B2) and `stance-adverbs` (B2) in news: *presuntamente*, *supuestamente*, *según*; plus the text and the Reading group.
Domain words: news, sources, events (e.g. *la fuente, el titular, la noticia, confirmar, desmentir, presunto, el portavoz, el suceso*); check each against the sheet first.
Text: a news report about an invented local event in an unnamed city (a fire in a warehouse, or a bridge closed after damage): what is confirmed, what officials said (unnamed: *el portavoz de los bomberos*), what is unconfirmed (in the rumour conditional), with a headline as the text's title. No real places, people, newspapers or figures that could be read as real data.
