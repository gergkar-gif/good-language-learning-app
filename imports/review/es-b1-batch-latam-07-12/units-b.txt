Facts you may use: José Antonio Páez led Venezuela after it left Gran Colombia (president from 1831, several times); Juan Manuel de Rosas governed Buenos Aires 1829–1832 and 1835–1852, defeated at Caseros (1852); Facundo Quiroga, caudillo of La Rioja, killed at Barranca Yaco in 1835; Sarmiento's *Facundo* (1845) set "civilización" against "barbarie" (his view: attribute it); Sarmiento was president of Argentina 1868–1874; Rafael Carrera dominated Guatemala from the late 1830s until his death in 1865. Caudillos led personal armies of rural men and ruled through loyalty and patronage. Present-day judgements stay out.

- -02 "Símbolos nacionales" (flags, anthems, heroes, statues): `pasiva-refleja` (B1 Unit 6: *se crearon himnos*).
- -03 "Crear una identidad": `subjunctive-value-judgments` (known; recap).
- -04 "Pueblos y fronteras" (the Paraguayan War, the War of the Pacific): `concesivas-aunque`.
- -05 "Imaginar la nación" (schools, history books, novels): now `correlativos-no-solo-sino`, taught in B1 Unit 40 (later): **re-point** to `ser-passive` (B1 Unit 18: *los héroes de la independencia fueron presentados como padres de la patria*); *no solo… sino* leaves the text, the screen, every item and option.
Facts: the Paraguayan War (War of the Triple Alliance, 1864–1870): Paraguay against Brazil, Argentina and Uruguay; Paraguay lost a very large part of its population (figures disputed: give none). The War of the Pacific (1879–1884): Chile against Peru and Bolivia; Bolivia lost its coast (Treaty of 1904 with Chile), Peru lost Tarapacá. National anthems: Argentina 1813, Mexico 1854 (first sung). Benedict Anderson's idea of the nation as an "imagined community" (1983) may be named as his. Novels used to build national identity, e.g. *María* (Jorge Isaacs, Colombia, 1867).

- -02 "Reformar el Estado" (Mexico's Reforma): `ser-passive` (B1 Unit 18).
- -03 "Iglesia y Estado": `se-impersonal` (B1 Unit 6).
- -04 "Modernizar la sociedad" (railways, ports, immigration): `gerundio-acciones-paralelas` (B1 Unit 2).
- -05 "Un nuevo modelo" (order and progress, the export model): `ordering-markers` (known; recap).
Facts: Mexico's Reforma: the Ley Lerdo (1856) forced the sale of corporate (Church and community) land, the 1857 constitution, the Reform War (1858–1861); Benito Juárez, a Zapotec, president; the French intervention and Maximilian, executed in 1867; the Porfiriato (Porfirio Díaz, 1876–1911, with one interruption 1880–1884). Positivism and "orden y progreso" (Brazil's flag, 1889, when Brazil became a republic). Mass European immigration to Argentina, Uruguay and southern Brazil from the 1870s; railways built largely with British capital. Selling indigenous community land left many peasants landless (historians' view: attribute).
