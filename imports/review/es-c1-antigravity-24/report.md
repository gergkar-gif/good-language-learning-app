## Norm claims

- `c1-24-01-a-gr`: *proponer que*, *sugerir que*, *solicitar que*, and *instar a que* govern the subjunctive when introducing a directive or proposal aimed at another party's action, whereas *señalar que* and *indicar que* take the indicative when reporting an established fact.
- `c1-24-02-a-gr`: *a fin de que*, *con el objeto de que*, and purpose *de modo que* require the subjunctive across distinct subjects, whereas *con miras a* and *con vistas a* take an infinitive (or noun phrase) when the subject is shared (or take *que* + subjunctive when subjects differ).
- `c1-24-03-a-gr`: After a past reporting verb (*sostuvo*, *aclaró*, *advirtió*, *anunció*), Spanish backshifts past/expired rounds (*presente -> imperfecto*, *pretérito/perfecto -> pluscuamperfecto*, *futuro -> condicional*, *imperativo/presente de subjuntivo -> imperfecto de subjuntivo*), while allowing the present indicative or present subjunctive when the speaker endorses an offer or request as still open at the time of reporting.
- `c1-24-04-a-gr`: *De haber + participio* and sentence-initial *Hubiera/Hubiese + participio* function as formal conditional protases without *si*, and *aun cuando + pluscuamperfecto de subjuntivo* expresses a counterfactual past concession, all pairing with the conditional perfect (*habría + participio*) in the apodosis.
- `c1-24-05-a-gr`: Discourse synthesis markers *por una parte ... por otra parte*, *en síntesis*, *cabe destacar que*, and *a modo de conclusión* take the indicative when stating factual conclusions or highlighting verified points, and combine with directive verbs + subjunctive when introducing a binding recommendation.
