## Segment 03 (lesson 03, challenge 03)
- Nurses' pay was raised gradually from 2019 | seg 03 | check
- Summer 2021: nurses demonstrated in Budapest for higher pay; a survey said more than a thousand nurses were considering working abroad | seg 03, L03, C03 | check
- Reports said some 400–500 nurses left the country each year | seg 03 | check
- October 2021: the government announced a 21 per cent nurses' pay rise from January 2022; further steps on 1 July 2023 and 1 March 2024; aim: professionals' average basic pay at 37 per cent of doctors' | seg 03 | check
- A WHO document said the nurse shortage could worsen as many retire | seg 03 | check
- Doctors' pay rose again in 2022 and 2023 after the 2021 rise | seg 03 | check
- The government said the rises improved professionals' position; the pay gap's assessment was contested | seg 03 | check

## Segment 04 (lesson 04, challenge 04)

- The state secretary said the law protected doctors, who could work without membership | seg 04 | check
- The law was submitted by Semjén Zsolt (deputy prime minister); in autumn 2020 the chamber was the government's negotiating partner | seg 04 | check
- Kincses Gyula, president of the chamber, called it an attack on the chamber | seg 04 | check
- The European Commission signalled that the law could endanger part of the EU funds | seg 04 | check
- By end of March 2023 more than 25,000 doctors said they would stay in the chamber | seg 04 | check
