# Norm research for ES C1 Units 3-7 (reviewer, 2026-11-18, from rae.es and secondary search excerpts)

- Rumour conditional (Unit 4): the RAE glossary (rae.es/gtg/condicional-de-modestia-condicional-de-rumor) records the term and says it refers to unconfirmed information, which therefore cannot be presented as news; Fundéu (Español Urgente, reproduced in El Caribe) advises moderation: it can be ambiguous and weaken credibility. A screen may say: common in the press, used for unconfirmed reports; style guides advise using it sparingly and naming the source. It must not call it an error.
- Future subjunctive (Unit 6): RAE glossary (rae.es/gtg/futuro-simple-de-subjuntivo): practically replaced by the present subjunctive, imperfect subjunctive or present indicative; survives as an archaic feature in legal and administrative texts, mainly in conditional and concessive clauses, and in ritual formulas, proverbs and fixed expressions (*sea cual fuere el precio*); the compound form (*hubiere registrado*) only in legal and administrative documents. NGLE (via UDEP): out of use in speech everywhere except some rural areas of the Canaries and the Caribbean. So "archaic and rare, still in legal/administrative texts" is right; "only in old laws" is too narrow.
- *el cual* (Unit 7): RAE Libro de estilo: in a restrictive relative clause without a preposition use *que*, not *el cual* (*Vi una casa que…*, not *la cual*); with a preposition *que* and *cual* forms are interchangeable (*a los que / a los cuales*); *cual* is required after a quantifier + *de* (*cuatro hijos, dos de los cuales son chicas*: *de los que* does not work there). Non-restrictive (after a comma) *el cual* is fine.
- Double participles (Unit 2, settled): see imports/review/es-c1-pilot/review.md.
