# Reviewer's facts, ES B1 core Units 53–63 (2026-11-12)

Read of the six unit stories before the readers' files arrived. Sources: es.wikisource.org texts saved in `sources/`; one web search for Darío's essay (no text of it could be fetched).

| b1-27-dondimas | The girl is "Angélica"; Dimas is poor, becomes rich and young, marries her; the devil leaves because "no condition was met" | Wrong. Palma: Dimas is a rich, miserly notary; the girl is Visitación; he offers his *almilla* for her love; the devil Lilit signs a three-year contract; at the end Dimas hands over his jacket (an *almilla* is a garment), is taken to hell, wins his case there and is sent back; Visitación has entered a *beaterio*. No youth, no marriage. | `sources/palma-don-dimas-de-la-tijereta-es.txt` (Tradiciones peruanas, first series) |
| b1-28-nuestraamerica | A government must grow from its own country; teach America's history first; learn from others without copying | In the essay ("El gobierno ha de nacer del país", "La historia de América, de los incas acá, ha de enseñarse al dedillo", "Injértese en nuestras repúblicas el mundo; pero el tronco ha de ser el de nuestras repúblicas"). Published 1891. Kept as reported ideas, unquoted. | `sources/marti-nuestra-america-es.txt` |
| b1-28-nuestraamerica | "Algunos críticos de la época pensaban que copiar las constituciones … era la manera más rápida de progresar" | Not in the essay as such (Martí attacks imported forms of government); to be reworded as what Martí argued against, or cut. | same |
| b1-29-exilio | Born Havana 1853; sent to Spain in 1871 aged 17; law and philosophy; Mexico, Guatemala, Venezuela; New York 1881; died 1895 in his first battle | Right in outline (born 28 January 1853; deported January 1871; first in New York 1880, settled 1881 after Venezuela; died at Dos Ríos, 19 May 1895). "Como castigo por sus ideas": he had first been sentenced to hard labour. | general reference knowledge, not re-fetched; dates given to the reader as the outline |
| b1-29-exilio | "hablaba de Cuba como si nunca se hubiera marchado" | Invented colour, and *como si* + pluperfect subjunctive is a later form: cut. | n/a |
| b1-30-andresbello | Born Caracas 1781; London, then Chile; grammar of 1847 for Americans; American usages are legitimate; unity of the language matters | In the prologue ("Mis lecciones se dirigen a mis hermanos, los habitantes de Hispano-América"; "Chile y Venezuela tienen tanto derecho como Aragón y Andalucía…"; the fear of a break-up like that of Latin). | `sources/bello-gramatica-prologo-es.txt` |
| b1-30-andresbello | Each nation has a right to "su propia manera de pronunciar"; the varieties are "ramas de un mismo árbol" | Neither is in the prologue: cut. | same |

Story to be rewritten to that outline; no text of the essay was available, so nothing is quoted. | web search 2026-11-12 (laprensani.com, cuadernosandinista.com, vanguardia.com.mx summaries) |
