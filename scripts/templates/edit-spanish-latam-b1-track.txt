## What this track is

Your units are on the B1 **Latin America** track (`latam`, es-latam only): 36 history units, from the pre-Columbian world to 2000, placed after B1 core Unit 36. The track is hidden in the app until it is read; it teaches **no new grammar skill**. Every lesson applies a grammar skill the learner already knows (A1–A2, or B1 core Units 1–36) to the unit's history, and teaches the topic's words. The learner knows only English and has finished A1–A2 and B1 core Units 1–36 (Units 37–40 are later). The track was generated in bulk and is in poor shape:

- **The texts are garbled and often about the wrong lesson.** Sentences are spliced from templates (a lower-case fragment after a full stop, a capital after a comma, the lesson's grammar frame stacked sentence after sentence, empty filler), and in many units each text covers another lesson's topic (the unit notes say where). **Every lesson text is rewritten from scratch.**
- Screens are in Spanish metalanguage around topic phrases that are not registry skills (*una vez que*, *a instancias de*); question stems are in Spanish; lesson titles are in Spanish; dialogue items invent speakers (*Marta*, *Pablo*, *Tomás*, *A*/*B*).
- Items, goals and listening sentences were written to match the garbled texts: they move with the new texts.

2. **Question stems in English.** Every multiple-choice and listening question asked in Spanish is asked in English. Reading questions ask about what the lesson's text says, in English, with Spanish options that are whole, short, untruncated statements (never options ending in "..."); one is right by the text as written, the others are clearly wrong by the text. A multiple-choice item takes neither `hint` nor `english`. The story file's `comprehensionQuestions` follow the same rule (English `question`, Spanish options, English `explanation`).

Inflected forms of a listed verb, and names, are fine. Keep each vocabulary list to the words the lesson really needs (about 8–14): prefer a met word over listing a rare one. Words listed in a lesson's vocabulary must be used in that lesson's text and items (`vocab-unused`): use each one or remove it from the list (never a lemma that `content/es-latam/conversation-scenarios.json` lists in `requiredLemmas`).
4. English everywhere the learner reads instructions: lesson `title` (English, with the subject: "The Maya: city-states in the rainforest"), `goal`, the `goal` and `checklist` sections (real can-do lines for this lesson, each distinct; never "Master 8 key vocabulary terms", never a grammar label in Spanish), any `intro` section (true about the track: screens and instructions are in English, the texts in Spanish).

## The texts (five lesson texts; the unit's combined reading is the reviewer's)

The lesson texts are `content/es-latam/stories/world/b1/<stem>-<slug>.json` (the path is in the lesson's `story` section; keep `id`, `level` and the file name; replace `title`, `paragraphs` and the questions). The unit's combined Library reading `<prefix>.json` is rebuilt from them by the reviewer (do not edit it). Non-fiction history, B1 Latin American Spanish:

- **About the lesson's own topic**, decided by the lesson title, goal and vocabulary list (not by the old text): in lesson order, no overlap with the neighbouring lessons beyond a linking sentence. 4–5 `narration` paragraphs, about 220–300 words, plain sentences a B1 learner can follow (one idea per sentence, few subordinate clauses deep), met words plus the lesson's listed topic words, each listed word used at least once in the meaning the list gives. The lesson's skill appears 2–3 times, naturally; never stacked sentence after sentence.
- **Facts.** Every date, figure, name and causal claim must be true. Keep a claim only if you are sure of it; when unsure, cut it or make it general ("a mediados del siglo XVI") rather than guess. Give no figure (population, death toll, distance, money) unless you are certain or the reviewer's facts in your notes confirm it. Never add a fact you are not sure of. Present-day claims (speakers of a language today, laws, institutions) are stated dated ("según el censo de 2020…") or left out.
- **Disputed matters** (the conquest, population collapse, slavery, the Black Legend, colonial violence): state what happened plainly, attribute judgements ("según muchos historiadores…", "Bartolomé de las Casas denunció…"), name a dispute in one line, and give no present-day political judgement in the narrator's voice. **No invented people and no invented quotations:** no named witness who speaks in quotation marks; a sentence in quotation marks must be a real, well-known quotation you are sure of, else paraphrase without quotation marks.
- **Language.** Clear, not academic: cut generator ornament (*formidable andamiaje burocrático*, *venas abiertas*, strings of abstract nouns). No talk about the text, the course or the reader (*este texto*, *el lector*, *como veremos*): `story-meta` must be 0. No asterisks in story files. Numbers under a hundred and centuries may be in words or figures, as the B1 core texts do; years in figures.
- `title` plain Spanish naming the lesson topic; three `comprehensionQuestions` per text (rule 2 above), each needing a specific fact of the text.
