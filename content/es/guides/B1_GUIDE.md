# Parlour B1 Content Guide

## 1. B1 Overview

B1 is the first fully independent intermediate stage of Parlour.

The B1 curriculum contains:

- **36 Core Language units**
- **6 classes per Core unit**
- **216 Core Language classes**
- **36 parallel Latin America lessons**
- approximately **32 new lexical items per dual unit**
- approximately **1,152 new lexical items across B1**
- a transition towards a predominantly Spanish-language app interface

B1 is organised as two parallel tracks:

1. **Core Spanish**: the main CEFR language progression.
2. **Latin America**: a historical/content course conducted in Spanish.

The two tracks appear side by side in the Lesson tab and progress together.

---

# 2. B1 Curriculum Architecture

Each B1 position contains a pair:

### Core Spanish
A language-focused unit containing approximately six classes.

### Latin America
A regional lesson connected to the same position in the learner's progression.

They do not need to cover the same subject.

The relationship is complementary:

> **Core Spanish = developing the language system**

> **Latin America = using Spanish to understand substantive content**

A Core unit may focus on expressing opinions while the corresponding Latin America lesson explores a historical political conflict.

Where useful, the two lessons can deliberately share vocabulary, grammar or communicative functions.

---

# 3. Core Spanish

## 36 Units

B1 Core contains **36 units**.

Each unit contains approximately **6 classes**, giving:

**36 × 6 = 216 classes**

The six-class structure provides enough space for:

1. Introduction
2. Development
3. Comprehension
4. Controlled practice
5. Active production
6. Review and consolidation

The exact balance can vary by unit.

The six classes should not become six repetitions of the same exercise type.

---

# 4. Core Vocabulary

A1 and A2 have already established the cumulative vocabulary base.

Current targets:

- A1: approximately **450 new words**
- A2: approximately **600 new words**
- cumulative at end of A2: approximately **1,050 words**

B1 should introduce approximately **1,152 new lexical items**.

### Per dual unit

Target approximately:

- **16 new Core lexical items**
- **16 new Latin America lexical items**

36 × 16 = **576 Core items**

36 × 16 = **576 Latin America items**

Total:

**1,152 new items**

This is a planning target, not a rigid quota.

Do not add artificial vocabulary simply to reach a number.

## Vocabulary should include

- individual words
- high-frequency expressions
- useful chunks
- collocations
- phrasal patterns
- topic-specific terminology

Collocations and fixed expressions should be treated as important learning units even when they are not counted as separate headwords.

## Recycling

New vocabulary must be repeatedly recycled through:

- later Core lessons
- Latin America lessons
- Workshop exercises
- reading
- listening
- translation
- writing
- cumulative review

A word introduced once is not considered learned simply because it appeared in a lesson.

---

# 5. B1 Grammar

B1 grammar should consolidate A1-A2 knowledge while introducing the structures needed for independent communication.

Core areas include:

### Verb system

- consolidation of present tense
- pretérito indefinido
- imperfecto
- contrast between past tenses
- present perfect
- future
- conditional
- common irregular forms
- verbal periphrases
- increasingly complex verb patterns

### Subjunctive

Development of the present subjunctive in contexts including:

- wishes
- recommendations
- necessity
- doubt
- emotion
- opinion
- evaluation
- purpose

Later B1 should begin preparing learners for more complex subjunctive structures.

### Sentence structure

- subordinate clauses
- relative clauses
- conjunctions
- connectors
- cause
- consequence
- purpose
- contrast
- condition
- comparison

### Pronouns

- direct object pronouns
- indirect object pronouns
- combined pronouns
- reflexive structures
- common pronoun placement patterns

### Other structures

- impersonal constructions
- passive and passive-like constructions
- reported speech foundations
- hypothetical structures
- comparisons
- common discourse structures

Grammar should serve communicative functions rather than exist as an isolated sequence of rules.

---

# 6. Core B1 Communicative Development

By the end of B1, the learner should be able to:

- narrate experiences and events
- describe change over time
- explain plans and intentions
- express and justify opinions
- agree and disagree
- compare alternatives
- give reasons
- explain causes and consequences
- discuss problems
- propose solutions
- describe experiences in greater detail
- participate in conversations on familiar social topics
- write connected texts
- understand the main points of clear spoken Spanish
- understand the main ideas and relevant details of accessible written Spanish

B1 should move progressively away from sentence-level production towards connected discourse.

# 10. Latin America Lesson Structure

A typical Latin America lesson should contain:

1. Historical or regional context
2. Key vocabulary
3. Spanish-language source text
4. Reading comprehension
5. Language observation
6. Listening or audiovisual material where appropriate
7. Content questions
8. Discussion or interpretation
9. Writing or production task
10. Review and retrieval

The regional course should feel like a genuine content course in Spanish.

It should not become a collection of simplified "facts about Latin America".

A typical Core unit contains approximately six classes.

A possible pattern is:

### Class 1: Introduce

- new language
- initial vocabulary

### Class 2: Develop
- grammar
- vocabulary
- controlled practice
- contextual examples

### Class 3: Read
- structured reading
- comprehension
- vocabulary in context
- language analysis

### Class 4: Listen
- listening comprehension
- spoken language
- retrieval
- interpretation

### Class 5: Practise
- Workshop integration
- translation
- sentence building
- gap fill

### Class 6: Use
- guided or freer production

# 12. Workshop Integration

Every B1 unit should provide material for the Workshop.

Relevant exercise types include:

- Verb Driller
- Grammar Driller
  - Sentence Builder
  - Gap Fill
  - Error Correction
- Translation
- Vocabulary Driller
  - Context
  - Collocations
- Listening Practice
- Writing Practice

The Workshop should increasingly require active retrieval and production.

B1 exercises should recycle both:

- Core Spanish content
- Latin America content

This creates a feedback loop between the Lesson and Workshop systems.

---

# 13. Reading

B1 should progressively move towards authentic Spanish.

Early B1 can use:

- graded texts
- adapted articles
- dialogues
- personal accounts
- accessible informational texts

Later B1 can use:

- authentic articles
- interviews
- reports
- opinion pieces
- historical texts
- accessible journalism
- Latin American sources

Texts should become longer and more linguistically varied over the level.

Difficulty should come from meaningful language and ideas rather than unnecessary obscure vocabulary.

---

# 14. Listening

Listening should progress from controlled speech towards increasingly natural Spanish.

Early B1:

- clear pronunciation
- familiar situations
- shorter recordings

Mid B1:

- interviews
- conversations
- reports
- multiple speakers

Late B1:

- natural conversational speed
- longer extracts
- authentic or lightly adapted material
- a wider range of accents

The Latin America track should begin introducing regional variation without making accent recognition the primary objective.

---

# 15. Writing

B1 writing should move from controlled sentences towards connected texts.

Possible tasks include:

- personal accounts
- descriptions
- emails
- messages
- opinion paragraphs
- explanations
- comparisons
- problem-solution texts
- short arguments
- summaries
- responses to source material

By the end of B1, learners should be producing coherent connected writing rather than collections of isolated sentences.

---

# 16. Spanish Interface Progression

The app interface should gradually become part of the Spanish learning environment.

### A1

English-supported interface.

### A2

English interface with increasing Spanish instructions and terminology.

### B1

Transitional bilingual interface.

Common interface labels and exercise instructions increasingly appear in Spanish.

Examples:

- Lesson → Lección
- Workshop → Taller
- Today's Focus → Enfoque de hoy
- Next Up → A continuación
- Complete the sentence → Completa la frase
- Choose the correct answer → Elige la respuesta correcta

English support can remain available where needed.

### B2

The interface becomes Spanish-first.

The B1 transition should prepare the learner for this without making navigation unnecessarily difficult.

---

# 17. Latin American Spanish

The Latin America track should primarily use broadly understandable Latin American Spanish.

Use:

- **ustedes** for plural 'you'
- broadly recognised Latin American vocabulary
- regional vocabulary where it is relevant to the topic
- authentic regional variation progressively

Do not use **vosotros** as the normal form in the Latin America course.

## Core Spanish variant

A1 and A2 never taught **vosotros** — every existing lesson, exercise and
story uses **tú** and **ustedes** only. Core B1's own authored material
(grammar examples, exercises, vocabulary, freshly written dialogue) follows
that same convention, for the same reason the Latin America track avoids
vosotros: testing a form nobody was ever taught breaks the "nothing is
asked for before it is taught" rule the whole course depends on.

This only bites where it wasn't obvious before: Core's adapted classics are
Peninsular Spanish, and some of them (Cervantes, in particular) would
naturally carry vosotros in their original dialogue. Where an adapted
extract's *source* dialogue authentically uses it, it may appear in the
**story text only**, as passive exposure — a learner reading, not
producing. It must never be the subject of an exercise (no fill-blank,
multiple-choice or dialogue-complete built around a vosotros form), and a
unit should not lean on it structurally. When adapting, normalising a
minor or ambiguous case to tú/ustedes is the safer default; only keep
vosotros where the original genuinely needs a group of listeners addressed
and normalising it would flatten the scene.

---

# 18. Review and Cumulative Learning

B1 should not be a sequence of isolated units.

Vocabulary and grammar must recur throughout the level.

Important language should return through:

- later lessons
- Workshop drills
- cumulative reviews
- translation
- reading
- listening
- writing
- Latin America content

Previously learned vocabulary should regularly appear in new contexts.

---

# 19. End-of-B1 Outcome

By the end of B1, the learner should be able to:

- communicate independently in common situations
- describe experiences and events
- discuss familiar social issues
- express and justify opinions
- explain causes and consequences
- discuss problems and solutions
- understand the main points of clear spoken Spanish
- read connected texts on familiar and moderately unfamiliar topics
- produce coherent connected writing
- use a substantially expanded vocabulary
- engage with Spanish-language historical and social content
- begin functioning in a Spanish-first digital environment

The learner should also have a basic historical understanding of Latin America sufficient to approach the B2 regional course.

---

# 20. B1 → B2 Transition

B1 ends with the learner having established:

**Core Spanish**
- approximately 36 units
- 216 classes
- approximately 576 new Core lexical items

**Latin America**
- historical content
- approximately 576 new regional/content lexical items
- coverage of the region up to approximately 2000

**Combined B1 vocabulary**
- approximately 1,152 newly introduced lexical items
- approximately 2,202 cumulative items when the A1 and A2 targets are included

B2 then changes the Latin America track from historical formation to:

> **Country-specific studies + contemporary regional themes**

The Core B2 track continues the language progression independently while the two tracks remain visually and pedagogically connected.

---

# 21. Design Principle

B1 is the stage where the learner begins using Spanish to understand more than their immediate personal environment.

**Core Spanish develops the language.**

**Latin America gives that language a substantive historical world.**

The two tracks should progress side by side, share vocabulary where useful, and feed the same Workshop and review systems.
